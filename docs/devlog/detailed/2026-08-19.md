# 2026-08-19 — Foliage that floated off its trunk

## What was believed, and proved false

**Believed:** `TreeCanopyLayerSpec.lift` positioned a canopy layer's lowest point above the trunk top, so `lift: 0.08` meant a deliberate 0.08 of daylight and `lift: 0` meant flush.
**False:** `lift` was applied to the layer's *nominal* height box (`centerY = trunkHeight + lift + height / 2`), and a faceted canopy does not fill that box. `DodecahedronGeometry(1, 0)` puts its lowest vertices at y = -phi/sqrt(3) ~= -0.934, not -1, so scaling by `height / 2` leaves the real geometry 3.3% of its height above the nominal floor — silently, in a direction nothing measured. The broadleaf's canopy started 0.100 above its trunk top instead of the intended 0.080, the columnar's 0.076 above instead of 0.040.

**Believed (by me, mid-task):** those two numbers were the size of the visual defect.
**False:** they understate it badly. A dodecahedron's bottom is a short *edge*, not a face — it tapers to roughly trunk width at its lowest point and only widens further up. Slice the broadleaf canopy at its trunk top and the cross-section is empty from every direction; slice it 0.02 higher and it is 0.158 wide facing the bottom edge but 0.040 wide facing perpendicular to it. The eye reads the joint where the foliage is actually as wide as the trunk, which is far above the bounding-box figure. This is why the screenshot gap looked two to three times the number I had computed.

## What a reviewer caught that the author missed

The reviewer here was the instrument check, not a person. The first version of the new contract test measured the **widest** point of the canopy's cross-section at the trunk top. Re-run against a deliberately shallow sink (broadleaf 0.10), that version reports 0.158 against a trunk radius of 0.075 and passes — while the tree still shows daylight through the joint from the perpendicular azimuth, where the same slice measures 0.040. Trees are randomly rotated per cell and the camera orbits freely, so "hidden from one angle" is not hidden. Switching the helper to the section polygon's **narrowest** side turns that same case red (`expected 0.0401898302980177 to be greater than 0.075`) and is what fixed the sink values that shipped.

Both halves of the new assertion were then checked for load-bearing: with every `canopySink` forced to 0 the test fails on "canopy reaches below the trunk top"; at broadleaf 0.10 it clears that and fails on the width instead.

## What the measurement moved, and from what

Canopy geometry, local units (trunk-top height / lowest canopy vertex / narrowest cross-section of the canopy at the trunk top, against that archetype's trunk radius):

| Archetype | Trunk top | Canopy floor, before | Canopy floor, after | Narrowest section at the joint, before -> after | Trunk radius |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| conifer | 0.34 | 0.340 (flush, coplanar) | 0.290 | 0.000 -> 0.318 | 0.055 |
| broadleaf | 0.48 | 0.580 (**0.100 of daylight**) | 0.410 | 0.000 -> 0.141 | 0.075 |
| columnar | 0.50 | 0.576 (**0.076 of daylight**) | 0.380 | 0.000 -> 0.085 | 0.060 |

The conifer was not visibly broken — a cone's base is a full-radius disc, so it read as joined — but it sat exactly coplanar with the trunk top, which is a seam waiting to happen rather than a joint. It now overlaps by 0.05 like the others.

Cost: each tree is shorter by its sink (conifer 0.05, broadleaf 0.15, columnar 0.16 of ~1.5-1.9 total), because seating foliage onto a trunk is exactly the operation of lowering it. Trunk heights are unchanged; the visible trunk is shorter by the sink.

## Notes for whoever touches this next

`createCanopyGeometry` now seats each layer by its **computed** lowest vertex (`computeBoundingBox()`), not by a shape-specific constant. Add a fourth canopy shape and it will be seated correctly without anyone deriving its bounding-box ratio. `lift` now means what its name says — height of this layer's lowest point above the canopy base — and `canopySink` is how far that base sits below the trunk top.

Park trees (`PARK_TREE_SPECIES` in `structure-style.ts`, drawn by `leisure-structures.ts`) are a **separate** implementation and were never affected: their canopy tiers are tubes whose `bottom` is already below the trunk's `trunkTop` (0.21 vs 0.26, 0.12 vs 0.17, 0.14 vs 0.19), so they overlap by construction. Verified visually alongside the fix, not assumed.

# 2026-08-19 (later) — Making the canopy defect a class the gates can see

User direction, verbatim: *"There should be a systematic way to check these things. Whenever I point out visual defects and bugs, make a record of it and find a way to always check them and potential new similar ones in the future. From multiple camera angles and zoom levels. Make this a fleet rule."*

## What was believed, and proved false

**Believed:** the canopy fix earlier the same day was verified from a close camera, roughly 1.15 world units from the tree.
**False:** `MapControls.minDistance` is 8 and `maxPolarAngle` is π/2.2. Every close-up taken through the game's own controls is an 8-unit view, cropped — the camera silently refuses to go nearer. The canopy gap was large enough to survive that, but a hairline joint like the school flag's 0.0017 is sub-pixel at 8 units and cannot be seen at all. Inspection now lifts both clamps.

**Believed:** setting `camera.position` and `controls.target`, then screenshotting a beat later, photographs that camera.
**False:** `CityScene` re-pins `controls.target.y` to the terrain every frame and shifts the camera by the same delta to preserve the angle. An aim point raised 0.44 above the ground therefore DROPS the camera by 0.44 before the shutter. Measured directly: set (106.4, **1.22**, 41.6) → photographed from (106.4, **0.78**, 41.6), with the ground at 0.86. That is why the first grazing frames were photographs of the underside of the map. The instrument now aims at ground level and expresses the angle purely as eye height, and reports the camera position read *after* the settle.

**Believed:** a part-level connectivity audit over `StructurePart[]` covers "a piece came off the model".
**False:** `ServiceModelFrame.part()` groups several primitives under one name and one bounding box. Floating a park lamp's globe 0.05 off its post is invisible at part granularity — the audit stayed green. It only goes red when every primitive is traced individually, which is what shipped.

## What a reviewer caught that the author missed

Again the instrument, not a person, and twice.

The primitive-tracing audit shipped with a completeness assertion — traced vertices must equal the builder's total — and that assertion failed immediately on 7 of 10 models, by 24 vertices each. The cause was double counting: primitives are built out of other primitives (a beam lays quad corners), so an outer traced call re-entered a traced method. Without that assertion the audit would have silently checked a superset it had miscounted, and nobody would have known which primitives were covered.

The audit's very first clean run then found a defect: the school flag hung off the flagpole's **circumradius**, which a 6-sided tube only reaches at a vertex. 0.0017 of daylight, sub-pixel, invisible — fixed anyway, because the alternative was widening the tolerance to 100× float noise.

## What the measurement moved, and from what

| Claim | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Composed models with a connectivity contract | 0 | 10 (6 services, 4 utility), primitive-level, with a coverage assertion |
| Camera distance available to inspection | clamped at 8 units | 0.02 (clamps lifted for the sweep only) |
| Camera position at shutter vs. at aim | differed by the aim offset, unmeasured | equal, and recorded per frame |
| Frames per full sweep | 1 screenshot, 1 angle | 88 frames, 11 contact sheets, 4 azimuths × 2–3 distances per subject |
| Grazing frames unusable (underground or inside foliage) | 2 of 4 on the first subject tried | 0 of 44; 11 frames lifted clear of foliage and each reports how far |

## Notes for whoever touches this next

`npm run visual:sweep` builds its own scene and photographs it; `CITY_SWEEP_SUBJECTS=school` narrows it while iterating. It is not a gate — it needs a browser and takes about a minute — and its automated checks only prove a frame drew geometry with no page errors. The contact sheets are the deliverable, and looking at them is the work.

The scene does **not** currently grow RCI buildings, which is the model a player sees most. Everything tried is written down in the register's "Known gaps" so the next attempt starts past it rather than repeating it.
