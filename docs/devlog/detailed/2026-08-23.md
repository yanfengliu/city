# 2026-08-23 — Per-type building panels, and an inspector that folds

## What was believed, and proved false

**Believed:** a section header carrying a one-line summary is free — it helps when the section is collapsed and is harmless when it is open.
**False, and visible the moment the panel was photographed rather than read as text.** A residential panel opened with the headline occupancy bar, then `OCCUPANCY  36 / 36 people` as the header summary, then the section's own occupancy bar with the same caption, then the lines. Three renderings of one fact before the first new sentence. The plain-text dump read as merely repetitive; the screenshot read as broken. Two fixes fell out: the summary renders only while the section is collapsed (it IS the collapsed view of the section), and the occupancy section dropped its meter because the headline bar above it already is that meter. A third followed: the section heading was renamed off the meter's own word — `Residents` / `Jobs & shoppers` / `Jobs` — so the panel's first two rows are never the same label.

**Believed:** the panel's live refresh could keep using the existing "rebuild the body when the content signature changes" path.
**False for a foldable panel.** The citizen panel refreshes twice a second while open. Rebuilding the section DOM on each refresh would discard the player's open/closed choices along with focus. The panel now reconciles section views by key and updates text in place, and collapse state lives in a map keyed by a section's stable `id` rather than its heading — headings carry live counts (`Household members (3)`), so heading-keyed state would silently reopen a section the moment its count moved.

**Believed (briefly, mid-task):** the sim's desirability score could be recomputed panel-side from the constants.
**Rejected before it shipped, and it is the design's load-bearing decision.** `buildingScore`, `buildingEducationOk`, and `nextLevelScore` were extracted out of `levelSystem` instead, so the panel and the system that levels and abandons buildings read the same arithmetic. The contract test asserts equality against `buildingScore` rather than against a literal, which is what makes it a check on agreement rather than a second copy of the formula.

## What the rendered result caught that the tests did not

Every test was green when each of these was live:

1. **Occupancy printed three times** (above).
2. **An abandoned building was warned about a countdown it had already lost** — `UTILITIES` showed `Abandonment risk 0 / 75 checks` and "Unsupplied for 0 of 75 checks before residents leave" on a shop that was already abandoned. The countdown and its bar are now suppressed once `abandoned` is true, and the sentence is worded by zone (`the residents leave` / `the shop closes` / `the factory closes`) instead of assuming a home.
3. **`Draws 1 units of each`** — a plural bug on a level-1 1×1 building, which is the most common building in a young city.
4. **`Everyone is home right now` in an empty building** — literally true of a building with zero households, and useless. It now says `Nobody lives here yet`.
5. **An abandoned building told to reach a threshold it already cleared** — "Desirability 36.5 — needs 12 to be used again" while the real blocker was the missing utilities named on the next line.

All five are now pinned by `tests/app/building-inspect.test.ts`.

## What a reviewer caught that the author missed

An independent critic — given the diff, the claims, and instructions to attack them — found three HIGH defects I had not, and disproved one claim outright. All three were the same shape: **the panel had quietly become a second opinion on a rule the sim already owns**, which is precisely the failure this design was built to prevent.

1. **The Growth section promised a level-up the sim will never make.** `levelSystem` `continue`s out of the entire level evaluation when the score is below the abandon line OR utilities are missing — so connected power and water are an unstated *precondition on levelling*, not merely a scoring bonus. The panel compared score against the threshold and reported "Level 2 threshold 45 is met · Levelling up: 0 of 3 good checks" with a full green progress bar, on a building the critic measured sitting at `level 1, upEvals 0` after 200 ticks with a score of 90 against a bar of 45. The one section whose job is to answer "why isn't this growing?" gave the wrong answer, and the real cause sat in a section the player may have collapsed. Fixed by computing a `growthBlocker` in `building-detail.ts` that retraces `levelSystem`'s branches **in its order**, and by suppressing the progress bar entirely while the level path is shut.
2. **Service coverage counted with a stricter metric than the sim's own.** `markCoverage` marks whole 4-cell blocks that intersect the radius box, so the sim credits coverage up to a block past the nominal radius; `coverageReach` filtered on exact Chebyshev distance. Measured: a home at distance 7 from a radius-6 garden shows the garden's ✅ and takes its +8 desirability, while the garden's own panel said "Serving 0 buildings and 0 residents". Two panels contradicting each other about the same pair of buildings. Fixed by exporting `coversCell` from `services.ts` — the marking rule itself — and consuming it.
3. **"Heading here" counted households already there, and households already walking home.** `citizen.shop` is set on departure and cleared only on arriving home, so it survives the dwell and the return leg; the filter checked only the id. Two households that had arrived and one on its way back all read as "3 households heading here to shop". Fixed by splitting on phase (`toShop` walking, `atShop` present) and reporting both — which turned out to be better copy than the original anyway. **The test I had written encoded the bug**: it seeded a household with no phase at all and asserted `visitors === 1`.

The same review confirmed, by measurement rather than by reading, the claim I most wanted checked: 610 `buildingScore().score` values across R/C/I over 600 ticks compared with `Object.is` against a byte-for-byte copy of the pre-extraction expression — **0 mismatches**.

It also found the panel had no automated DOM coverage at all, because this repo had no jsdom. That was true and worth fixing rather than arguing with: `jsdom` is now a devDependency and `tests/ui/inspect-panel-dom.test.ts` pins 21 rendered behaviours, including the three that only exist across a refresh — collapse state per subject, focus retention, and node reuse. Three deliberate re-breaks (append instead of insert, drop the per-subject reset, always show the summary) each turn it red.

## Verification

Real synthetic input through the harness `PlayerInput`, in a headless Chromium against the dev server — 8/8 checks, zero console errors:

- Escape with Zone R armed returns to Select; Escape mid-drag drops the drag and KEEPS the road tool, and the second press returns to Select; Escape with Select already active closes the open inspector.
- A real click on a home opens `Home — Level 1`; a real click on a coal plant's 3×3 footprint opens `Coal power plant` (this exercises picking, not just the tool call).
- The coverage square appears only while a service is selected, and clears when the selection moves to a home.
- Bulldozing the inspected building closes its panel rather than stranding it.
- A collapsed section stays collapsed across a live refresh (`aria-expanded` false, panel text shorter).

Panel screenshots were captured for all ten subjects (R/C/I, four services, coal, wind, pump) plus the resident panel open and collapsed, and reviewed.

## The scaffold defects worth remembering

Two of the three failures during verification were in the harness, not the product, and both would have read as product bugs from the log alone: a hardcoded 3×3 plant site landed in a lake (so `utilityCellOwner` was empty and the cell arithmetic produced `NaN`), and a service placed one row too far from the road was refused. The refusal message — "no cell of the footprint at (11, 3) touches a road — services must sit beside one" — is what identified it immediately, which is the error-messages-as-a-product-surface rule paying for itself inside the repo's own test scaffolding.
